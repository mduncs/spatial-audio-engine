# A-10 checkpoint gun run

## V1 scene model

The checkpoint uses two ordinary looping WAV sources with an identical 84.000 s timeline. `squad-a10-pass` is the sky perspective. Its wide Flyby recording begins at 28.136 s, its FlybyClose layer begins at 39.136 s, the distant GAU-8 report begins at 39.959 s, and the close-pass envelope peaks at 43.636 s. Those sky events are authored 0.364 s early to offset the engine's direct travel time from the static 125 m-high proxy to the strike point, giving intended target arrivals at 28.500, 40.323, and 44.000 s. The wide recording carries the source-file egress through 77.009 s, leaving 6.991 s of silence before the loop boundary. `squad-a10-impacts` starts four Close-pool impact variants at 38.000, 38.180, 38.360, and 38.540 s in fixed 01, 02, 03, 04 order. Each variant occurs once, so no repeat is adjacent. Their overlapping tails make one ground-strike cluster.

The rip differs slightly from the library inventory: `Game/Vehicles/A10/Sounds/Flyby/` contains `01`, `02`, and `05`, not five files. The composition uses `A10_flyover_01.ogg`, whose envelope has one clear dominant pass, plus `A10_flyby_close_02.ogg` and `gau9_fire_far_01.ogg`. The impact stem uses all four `gau9/Impacts/Close` variants. Mid and Far impacts are excluded because they bake in distance character that the engine already supplies.

The sky source is static at `[292.5, 342.5, 127.0]`, directly above the DShK strike point and 125 m above it. The impacts are centered at `[292.5, 342.5, 2.0]` with a 35 m line segment. Workbench source forward is local north, so that extent lies along the north-south street and represents the strafed strike line rather than a point explosion. Both sources are disabled by default.

## Timing and speed compensation

The public A-10 demonstration profile is a useful low-level reference, though it is not a combat-employment prescription. The U.S. Air Force's A-10 pop-up strafe table specifies a 300 ft AGL entry at 325 KCAS, about 91 m and 167 m/s, with a 1,500 ft apex and 200 ft exit. The authored 125 m height sits in the requested representative 100-150 m AGL band, and 167 m/s sits in the requested 150-180 m/s band. See [AFMAN 11-246V1, section 3.21 and table 3.14](https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/af_a3/publication/afman11-246v1/afman11-246.pdf).

For the impacts-first signature, the timing model uses a 1,200 m slant range and the published 1,021 m/s muzzle velocity of the PGU-13 HEI cartridge. Ignoring drag, projectile time is `1,200 / 1,021 = 1.175 s`; muzzle-report time at 343 m/s is `1,200 / 343 = 3.499 s`. Using the unrounded quotients, the impact precedes the report by 2.323 s. The impacts arrive at the strike point at 38.000 s. The report begins in the sky WAV at 39.959 s, then the engine adds approximately `125 / 343 = 0.364 s` from the static proxy, producing the intended 40.323 s target arrival. At 125 m AGL, the firing slant range corresponds to `sqrt(1,200^2 - 125^2) = 1,193 m` horizontally; at 167 m/s the aircraft covers that in about 7.15 s. This agrees with the physical firing instant at 36.825 s and the target-arrival close-pass peak at 44.000 s to within 0.04 s. Projectile data are from the [U.S. Army JPEO Armaments & Ammunition portfolio, PGU-13B/B entry](https://jpeoaa.army.mil/Portals/94/JPEOAA/Documents/JPEOAAPortfolioBook_2017.pdf).

The Flyby and FlybyClose files are already ground-observer recordings of an aircraft at real speed. They contain their own Doppler sweep, trajectory impression, level evolution, and pass character. Giving that audio a 167 m/s runtime trajectory would apply motion twice. It would also exceed the current fractional-delay slew cap in `render.rs`: 0.01 samples per output sample corresponds to only about `0.01 * 343 = 3.43 m/s` of sustained radial speed. V1 therefore lets the recording carry motion while the engine contributes source placement, direct-path occlusion, and reflections.

The probe layers end at 63 m, below the 127 m sky source. It receives no path-stage fill and uses direct plus reflections only. A source more than 100 m above these streets is effectively line of sight for this scene, so that limitation is acceptable for V1. Once fast-mover rendering lands, the replacement should use a dry, steady jet loop on a true north-south trajectory, let the engine compute propagation delay and Doppler, and keep impacts as a separate ground source.

## Level anchors

U.S. Air Force data reproduced in the 2006 Military Training Route EA list the A-10 at 96.9 dBA SEL and 93.2 dBA Lmax at a 1,000 ft (304.8 m) slant distance. See [Appendix E, table E.1-2](https://www.nepa.navy.mil/Portals/20/Documents/Pacific%20Fleet/GOA/files/EIS/Final_EIS_2011/references/MTR_EA/Appendix_E_Sound_Basics.pdf). Because the engine's source control represents program RMS, not an event maximum, the sky stem uses a cycle-energy anchor: `96.9 - 10*log10(84) = 77.66 dBA Leq,84s` at 304.8 m, then `77.66 + 20*log10(304.8) = 127.34 dB` at 1 m. The fixture rounds this to 127 dB SPL at 1 m. For context, directly back-solving the published Lmax gives 142.88 dBA at 1 m, but assigning that maximum as the 84 s program RMS would overstate the whole bed.

Shaw and Gee measured the GAU-8 outdoors above 3,000 Pa, or 163 dB peak, at 30 ft (9.1 m). See [their 2010 Noise Control Engineering Journal paper](https://physics.byu.edu/download/publication/632), DOI `10.3397/1.3495736`. A free-field back-solve would be about 182 dB peak at 1 m, but that is a muzzle-blast maximum, not a continuous bed level and not an impact measurement. The sky stem therefore retains the Far firing recording's authored crest within the 127 dB cycle bed. The impact stem uses 163 dB at 1 m as a deliberately conservative per-burst control, without the 19 dB back-solve. Like the existing M2 and DShK controls, this exposes an impulsive reference through a steady-source field; it does not claim continuous physical SPL, exact impact SPL, or delivered-ear SPL.
